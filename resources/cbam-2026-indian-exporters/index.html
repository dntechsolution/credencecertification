CBAM 2026: What Indian Exporters Must Do Before the 30 September 2027 Declaration

The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism entered its definitive period on 1 January 2026. For Indian plants exporting covered goods to Europe and the UK, 2026 production records directly determine what your buyers pay.

01 · The 2026 Shift

Why the definitive period changes buyer behaviour

During the transitional phase, reporting focused on data submission without certificate surrender. From 1 January 2026, authorised EU CBAM declarants incur direct financial liability for the carbon embedded in imported steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity. Importers can only apply an Indian manufacturer's actual embedded emissions if those figures are independently verified. Without verified actuals, buyers fall back on European Commission default values—which include rising mark-ups (+10% in 2026, +20% in 2027, +30% from 2028 onward).

02 · Action Plan for Indian Plants

Six steps to complete before your buyer asks

  1. 1. Confirm CN code applicability: Map every exported SKU to its 8-digit Combined Nomenclature code and confirm whether it falls under CBAM or downstream scope.
  2. 2. Lock installation & process boundaries: Document production routes, aggregated goods categories, fuel inputs and process emissions consistently across the 2026 calendar year.
  3. 3. Audit metering & calibration logs: Verify that weighbridges, fuel meters and electricity sub-meters are calibrated and reconciled with purchase invoices.
  4. 4. Collect precursor emissions data: Obtain documented embedded emissions from upstream suppliers of clinker, billets, hot metal, alumina or chemical precursors.
  5. 5. Substantiate electricity emission factors: Separate grid, captive and PPA/renewable power consumption with auditable utility and contract records.
  6. 6. Run a pre-verification readiness assessment: Identify data gaps in a mock audit now, before formal accredited verification is required for the 30 September 2027 declaration.

Accreditation Transparency

Under EU CBAM rules, final verification of actual emissions for an importer's declaration must be conducted by a verifier accredited by an EU/EEA National Accreditation Body.
